CBT · Between-session support

A thought is a starting point, not a verdict

CBT helps people notice links among thoughts, feelings and actions, then test more helpful ways of responding.

  1. Choose a mild everyday situation to practice with. Describe what happened without deciding what it means.
  2. Name the feeling and the first thought that appeared. Ask what supports the thought and what might point to another explanation.
  3. Write a balanced response and choose one small useful action. Bring what you learned to your therapist.

For example: A friend has not replied. “They may be busy; I do not yet know why.” I can continue my activity and check in later.

Plan with your therapist: A short thought record supports therapy; it is not a complete CBT course. Do not dismiss real adversity or interpret every distressing thought as inaccurate.
